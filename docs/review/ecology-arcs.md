# Ecology, encounter arcs, and power contrast — J081–J100

These questions test whether creatures and exceptional encounters derive power from world facts and
real capabilities rather than player-level scaling, arbitrary boss flags, or generated prose. Use a
fixed world/site snapshot, actual instantiated mechanics, and durable lifecycle fixtures.

Sources: [ecology](../systems/ecology.md), [encounters](../systems/encounters.md),
[arc generation](../systems/arc-generation.md), [reservoir example](../systems/reservoir-example.md),
and [validation](../systems/validation.md).

## Ecology and independent power

- [ ] **J081.** In a fixed world snapshot, does an ordinary creature's capability remain independent of the player's rank, loadout, and recent wins or losses?
  - **Evidence:** generation inputs and paired player-state runs over the same site facts.
  - **Falsifier:** an established creature is rewritten to track the player.
- [ ] **J082.** For an exceptional specimen, can each extraordinary capability be traced to lineage potential, individual history, resources, habitat, or a supported site relationship?
  - **Evidence:** capability provenance and readable in-world evidence.
  - **Falsifier:** a boss-only power appears with no supported prerequisite or cost.
- [ ] **J083.** In an old but ordinary region and a young but dangerous site, do age and distance communicate context without acting as direct damage multipliers?
  - **Evidence:** matched sites, capability records, and player-facing clues.
  - **Falsifier:** age or distance alone guarantees stronger combat stats.
- [ ] **J084.** After repeated deaths advance game time, does the ecology avoid automatically promoting every nearby population beyond reach?
  - **Evidence:** long time-jump fixture with deterministic ecological support and local population outcomes.
  - **Falsifier:** elapsed time universally escalates danger without bottlenecks or world-specific support.
- [ ] **J085.** If a creature's displayed trait or known capability is used in an encounter, does the actual phenotype and instantiated mechanics support it?
  - **Evidence:** phenotype, creature record, clue, and combat behavior for the same entity.
  - **Falsifier:** presentation claims a power the creature cannot actually use.

## Dominance, warning, and apex fairness

- [ ] **J086.** In a mature-character route through ordinary inhabitants, can the player demonstrate earned dominance without each peon requiring a long repetitive combat sequence?
  - **Evidence:** resolved turns, action count, and meaningful area/multi-target outcomes.
  - **Falsifier:** ordinary foes remain synchronized damage sponges or consume a mandatory consumable tax.
- [ ] **J087.** Before an irreversible encounter commitment, does the site expose evidence that the apex is exceptional and provide a plausible way to observe, prepare, or retreat?
  - **Evidence:** actual clue order, player viewport, and commitment transition.
  - **Falsifier:** unavoidable lethal danger appears without warning or escape opportunity.
- [ ] **J088.** In the same apex phase, can its capability cycle be read from telegraphs and state changes rather than guessed from inflated Health or unexplained damage?
  - **Evidence:** phase rules, screen-sized presentation, and action-resolution log.
  - **Falsifier:** success depends on hidden timing or arbitrary stat magnitude.
- [ ] **J089.** Across the intended build fixtures, does the immutable apex present different exploitable opportunities without requiring equal win rates or a single hidden key?
  - **Evidence:** per-build approaches and capability requirements against the same encounter definition.
  - **Falsifier:** one exact item/build is mandatory, or fairness is implemented as identical outcomes.
- [ ] **J090.** When a character cannot yet defeat the apex, can it pursue a useful alternate objective or retreat with enough information to form a credible return plan?
  - **Evidence:** alternate reward/access conditions and partial-site lifecycle.
  - **Falsifier:** an underprepared player must either die or abandon all meaningful progress.
- [ ] **J091.** When an advanced character returns to a previously dangerous site, does the encounter remain fixed while ordinary inhabitants can be meaningfully easier?
  - **Evidence:** before/after site definition and actor capability records.
  - **Falsifier:** the site secretly scales upward or deletes the evidence of the character's growth.
- [ ] **J092.** Can players disengage during each supported apex phase, with persistent actor and objective state matching the declared retreat rules on return?
  - **Evidence:** retreat/revisit fixtures at each phase.
  - **Falsifier:** retreat deletes progress, rerolls the apex, or gives a free reset without a rule.

## Drafting facts and validating arcs

- [ ] **J093.** For a drafted arc, can every referenced placement, actor, capability, resource, clue, and reward be traced to the bounded factual snapshot or permitted catalog?
  - **Evidence:** accepted draft and reference-resolution report.
  - **Falsifier:** model-authored prose makes an invented fact mechanically true.
- [ ] **J094.** If a site's terrain or candidate actors change after a draft snapshot, does validation reject or refresh stale references before the arc can be accepted?
  - **Evidence:** changed-revision acceptance fixture.
  - **Falsifier:** stale placements or capabilities are committed into the world.
- [ ] **J095.** Does mechanical validation recompute threat and reward legality from actual core rules instead of trusting the draft's difficulty label or prose claim?
  - **Evidence:** budget recomputation and deliberately understated draft fixture.
  - **Falsifier:** a declared “easy” label makes an over-budget encounter pass.
- [ ] **J096.** In an arc with optional clue ordering and alternate entrances, can the dependency graph remain satisfiable without turning every beat into a mandatory kill gate?
  - **Evidence:** dependency graph plus out-of-order and alternate-route fixture.
  - **Falsifier:** a narrative prerequisite blocks a physically valid or observational approach without an authored rule.
- [ ] **J097.** Before the apex commitment, does every required key, consumable, access tool, and warning have an available source not locked behind itself?
  - **Evidence:** reachability and resource-source validation.
  - **Falsifier:** an unsatisfied circular dependency makes the arc impossible.
- [ ] **J098.** Can model-produced names or prior draft text that contain instructions remain inert data rather than changing the schema, catalog, or validation policy?
  - **Evidence:** adversarial input fixture with instruction-like text and unchanged authority boundaries.
  - **Falsifier:** untrusted text alters mechanics or overrides the allowed catalog.
- [ ] **J099.** Once an arc is revealed, can a late remote response or duplicate accepted response neither replace its mechanics nor duplicate its rewards?
  - **Evidence:** late-response, duplicate-delivery, and reward-claim lifecycle tests.
  - **Falsifier:** timing or duplicate delivery rewrites a revealed site or pays twice.
- [ ] **J100.** For an apex and surrounding arc, do clue prose, structured beats, and actual mechanics agree on the promised capabilities, warning, opportunities, and aftermath?
  - **Evidence:** prose-to-rule review and played accepted-arc fixture.
  - **Falsifier:** the narrative promises an unsupported interaction or omits a mechanically decisive fact.
