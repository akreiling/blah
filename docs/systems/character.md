# Character development

[Design index](../SYSTEMS_ROADMAP.md) · [Decisions](decisions.md) · [Checkpoints](checkpoints.md)

Proposed design. See the decisions document for committed direction versus prototype rules.

## Five distinct concepts

| Concept | Meaning | Changes through | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | A developing capacity such as force, channeling, or precise control | Related meaningful practice | Diminishing returns and adaptation-dependent ceilings |
| Adaptation | A lasting structural development of the character | An explicit, earned breakthrough | Finite structural capacity and incompatibilities |
| Proficiency | Familiarity and reliability in an activity | Performing that activity effectively | Task difficulty and relevant experience |
| Technique | A specific action or modified action the character knows | Practice, discoveries, instruction, experimentation | Foundation requirements and composition rules |
| Integration | Ability to coordinate techniques or maintain one while doing another | Practicing the relationship in real situations | Attention, sustain capacity, and specific compatibility |

These concepts should not become five overlapping currencies. Each needs a distinct job. If a prototype
cannot explain that job in the UI, simplify the representation instead of adding more meters.

A **discipline**, also called a track in discussion, is a coherent family of practices and rules spanning
these concepts. It is not a sixth advancement resource. A **weave** is a practiced integration that produces
a combined technique or coordinated effect across disciplines.

## Starting foundations

The first prototype uses these working categories:

| Foundation | Supports | Does not automatically imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Force | Physical impulse, heavy weapon handling, forceful movement | Melee-only play or a heavy-armored identity |
| Resilience | Endurance, carrying strain, maintaining action under pressure | Magical immunity or limitless Health |
| Channeling | Moving and sustaining magical power | Long range, quiet casting, or healing expertise |
| Precision | Fine control of strikes, projectiles, extraction, and shaped magic | Stealth or a particular weapon family |
| Awareness | Reading motion, detecting threats, tracking, and anticipating openings | Omniscience or knowing undiscovered traits |
| Subtlety | Controlling detectable movement, sound, and magical expression | Physical weakness or a ban on overt attacks |

Names and grouping are prototype choices. Do not add a foundation merely because a conventional RPG has a
corresponding attribute.

Range and delivery are practiced relationships, not moral or class categories. Precision can support a
blade, a shuriken, a short-range magical burst, or delicate gathering. Channeling can support close combat
and protection as readily as projectiles.

## Structural capacity

The character has a finite capacity for major adaptations. Capacity can expand at selected milestones, but
not without bound and not enough to fit every high-tier adaptation.

An adaptation has:

- A visible benefit.
- The activities and situations that made it available.
- A structural cost.
- Any specific incompatible adaptations.
- A clear account of what development it enables or makes harder.

Examples:

| Adaptation | Enables | Commitment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concentrated channeling | Large bursts at short distance | Deep investment in burst delivery rather than effortless distant projection |
| Distributed control | Coordinating several independent projectiles | Consumes control capacity that could support a single exceptionally concentrated effect |
| Anchored protection | Strong sustained wards under pressure | Maintaining maximum protection restricts movement or other sustained work |
| Veiled expression | Suppressing detectable magical output | Quiet execution costs time, Focus, or peak output |

These are illustrative adaptations, not a complete catalog or numerical specification.

The limiting mechanism cannot be only a rising experience cost. If an old character can eventually buy every
adaptation with enough grinding, all characters converge. Finite structure prevents that convergence while
leaving a long refinement path inside the chosen identity.

## Early agency and later commitment

Early actions produce evidence, not silent permanent commitments. The player can try weapons, basic magic,
quiet movement, and gathering before a major choice.

When a breakthrough becomes available:

1. The UI describes what the character has been practicing.
2. It presents a small set of developments justified by that history.
3. It shows costs, incompatibilities, and a preview of the playstyle each supports.
4. The player can accept a development or defer the commitment.
5. Nothing irreversible happens because of an accidental input or an opaque hidden threshold.

There is no general-purpose respec in the selected prototype. A future exceptional transformation would
require a separate design decision; it must not quietly become a routine way around identity.

## Nearby branches versus reinvention

| Change | Expected difficulty |
| --- | --- |
| Learn another useful technique for the same weapon | Accessible practice |
| Move from shuriken to another thrown projectile | Related proficiency plus equipment-specific practice |
| Add a small protective technique to a channeling character | Plausible adjacent development |
| Develop quiet versions of magic while remaining capable of overt casting | A meaningful specialization |
| Turn a mature distributed-projectile specialist into a concentrated close-range blaster | Major structural conflict; not a loadout swap |
| Become a completely different physical, magical, or subtle identity | Normally a new character |

Skills do not decay just because they are not used. An old skill can remain known while being impractical
under the character's current structural and equipment constraints.

## Godlike refinement

Late development expands what a specialist can accomplish:

- Scale: more targets, a wider area, a longer reach, or a stronger intervention.
- Control: finer targeting, fewer unwanted effects, better timing.
- Coordination: maintaining a complex interaction while performing another compatible action.
- Reliability: executing under conditions that would previously disrupt it.
- Authority: interacting with larger or more powerful world features.

Refinement does not improve every dimension simultaneously. A larger area may reduce precision. Sustained
protection may consume attention. Greater range may reduce concentration or require a longer commitment.

Power should increase enough that ordinary threats stop demanding careful play. The meaningful constraints
remain visible against exceptional threats.

Refinement is not always a sidegrade. A master can be strictly stronger and more capable than their earlier
self in the same situation. Trade-offs constrain the competing extremes of current power; they must not
cancel every upgrade so thoroughly that "godlike" becomes a cosmetic claim.

## Levels and overall rank

Do not add a generic level as an independent source of stats or skill points in the first implementation.
The development system already owns advancement.

If an overall rank is useful for communicating maturity, derive it from committed development. It must not:

- Scale enemies.
- Replace technique prerequisites.
- Make magic or physical development interchangeable through generic point spending.
- Become the only measure of whether a character can solve an encounter.
