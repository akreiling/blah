# Vision, inspirations, and game loops

[Design index](../SYSTEMS_ROADMAP.md) · [Decisions](decisions.md) · [Checkpoints](checkpoints.md)

Proposed design. See the decisions document for committed direction versus prototype rules.

## The promise

> The world changes what you do. What you do changes who you become.

Creatures grow into what their environments reward. The wanderer grows into the activities, combinations,
and difficult situations they repeatedly practice. Neither should be an arbitrary collection of independent
numbers.

The player begins with broad potential. Over time, that potential becomes a particular structure:
aptitudes, adaptations, practiced combinations, and signature techniques. In the endgame, advancement refines
real, extraordinary power. It does not turn every old character into the same universal master.

The power fantasy is genuine:

- A veteran can walk into lesser creatures and destroy them.
- Earlier dangers really do become manageable.
- Exceptional foes can become just as extraordinary as the player.
- A major encounter builds toward an apex that forces the player to use their power intelligently.
- The game does not quietly adjust ordinary enemies to match the player's level.

The central pleasure is not only learning facts about wildlife. It is using that knowledge, developing a
particular kind of power, finding tools that extend it, and confronting things worth becoming powerful for.

### What makes this different from a conventional RPG

Genetics cannot be wallpaper behind ordinary combat. A creature's traits must change useful choices:
approach, distance, weapon behavior, spell effect, stealth route, protection, timing, and escape.

Likewise, a classless character cannot just be a set of unrelated progress bars. Repeatedly using skills
together must create recognizable combinations. The combination has a playstyle, not merely a larger menu.

An old character's history should be visible in what they can do:

> This person did not select "battlemage thief." They grew into one.

### The progression arc

| Stage | Central question | Primary reward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potential | What might I become? | Trying useful actions and noticing promising combinations |
| Identity | Which ways of acting am I committing to? | Coherent strengths and meaningful developmental commitments |
| Power | What can I now accomplish that others cannot? | Capabilities that change encounters and access |
| Refinement | How far can I take this particular power? | Extraordinary control, scale, expression, and new trade-offs |

These are design stages, not four mandatory UI ranks or a promise of fixed playtime per stage.

### Inspirations and what to borrow

- **Gath of Baal / the Death Dealer:** use the reference for formidable physical presence, a recognizable
  identity, and physical might intertwined with supernatural power. A raging warrior protected by powerful
  magic should feel like an embodied force, not a fragile wizard wearing the wrong equipment. This is a tonal
  and power-fantasy reference, not a requirement to copy the character, artifacts, plot, or involuntary loss
  of player control.
- **Lyndon Hardy's _Master of the Five Magics_:** borrow distinct magical disciplines with understandable
  laws and powerful interactions between them. Do not reduce magic to differently colored projectiles.
  Do not borrow universal mastery as the mandatory destination of every character.
- **EVE:** borrow the long horizon, meaningful specialization, and fitting constraints. Do not borrow
  real-time training, offline progression, or the assumption that every mature identity belongs on one
  character eventually.
- **Creature genetics:** borrow development into a coherent, particular expression of potential. The
  character's learned structure should be as meaningful as a creature's body and adaptations.

Reference context: [Gath in the Death Dealer novels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Dealer_(painting)#Novels)
and [Hardy's disciplines and their combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_the_Five_Magics).
These references identify the inspirations. The mechanical design below is this game's proposal, not a
claim that either work uses these exact rules.

### Non-goals

- A museum of procedural creatures with little reason to interact with them.
- A standard loot treadmill with unusual monster names.
- Freely changing a mature character's identity by swapping a loadout.
- A real-world training queue, offline advancement, or idle-time mastery.
- Making repeated safe actions the optimal route to power.
- A universal endgame build that does everything simultaneously.
- Uniformly level-scaled enemies that erase earned superiority.
- An unrestricted procedural spell language or model-generated executable mechanics.
- A fully simulated ecosystem before one encounter is interesting.
- Building every system in this document before the next playtest.

## The game's loops

### Moment-to-moment loop

1. Observe a situation.
2. Form a prediction from terrain, creature traits, awareness, and available tools.
3. Choose an action or sequence.
4. See an understandable result.
5. Adapt, commit, or withdraw.

The player should be able to explain why a tactic worked. Uncertainty is valuable; arbitrary outcomes are not.

### Journey loop

1. Notice or choose a destination.
2. Prepare a loadout that expresses the character's identity.
3. Travel through a mix of ordinary wildlife and meaningful obstacles.
4. Gather supplies, encounter unfamiliar variants, and find evidence of larger threats.
5. Decide whether to continue, investigate, take a detour, or return.
6. Acquire treasure, experience, and opportunities for development.
7. Recover, commit new growth, and choose a new ambition.

Preparation must matter without turning every outing into inventory administration.

### Character loop

1. Try a useful activity.
2. Practice it in meaningful situations.
3. Combine it with existing strengths.
4. Receive visible evidence of development.
5. Choose a supported adaptation or technique development.
6. Use the new capability to encounter situations previously out of reach.

The choice in step 5 steers growth. It does not replace the lived history that made the choice available.

### Long-term ambition loop

1. Discover evidence of a power beyond the character's present ability.
2. Learn enough to understand the gap without knowing every answer.
3. Pursue relevant refinement, equipment, and world knowledge.
4. Return with a better plan.
5. Defeat, outmaneuver, or otherwise resolve the encounter.
6. See a durable consequence and a reward worthy of the effort.

Not every encounter must be winnable when first found. Discovering something too powerful can create a goal,
provided its warning and retreat opportunities are fair.

### Replayability loop

1. Encounter a technique, creature behavior, relic, or approach your current identity does not express.
2. Understand enough of it to imagine a different way of playing.
3. Continue valuing the current character's distinct strengths.
4. Develop a concrete idea for another character.
5. Start again with different early practices and commit along that direction.
6. Discover differences beyond the initial idea rather than follow an identical route with different damage.

The desired thought is "Next time I want to become that," not "I picked wrong and have to repeat the
tutorial." A finite structural future makes the next character meaningful; fairness and early useful
experimentation keep that limit from becoming punishment.
